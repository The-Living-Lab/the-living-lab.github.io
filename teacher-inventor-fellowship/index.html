Teacher-Inventor Fellowship

Turn a problem from your own classroom into a working AI-enabled tool.

The Living Lab’s new fellowship pairs K–12 teachers with a Penn GSE graduate fellow to build an AI-enabled tool for a real need in their own classroom. No AI or coding experience is required.

Apply now

Apply for yourself or nominate a fellow educator. Takes about 15 minutes.

The program

Who
U.S. K–12 classroom teachers, any subject or grade
When
10 weeks from January 2027 · 2–3 hours a week
Format
Mostly online · optional sessions in Philadelphia
Support
A Penn GSE graduate fellow builds with you
Funds
Up to $1,000 for costs related to your invention
Experience
No AI or coding experience required

Who we’re looking for

We welcome K–12 teachers from all kinds of schools across the United States: public, charter, or independent; urban, suburban, or rural; and at any level of experience with AI. You don’t need to know what you’ll build yet. We’re looking for real problems in your practice, not a finished solution.

  • Have a problem you care about. Something in your practice you want to solve.
  • Are curious about AI and willing to question it. Enthusiasm and skepticism are both useful.
  • Are interested in trying things in your teaching, including testing the invention with students or in your own practice.
  • Are willing to share in the open, including what didn’t work.

Eligibility

  • Current U.S.-based K–12 classroom teachers, any subject, any grade level
  • No technical or programming background required
  • Support from your principal or school leader is encouraged but not required
  • Prior participation in programs such as PASS (Pioneering AI in School Systems) is welcome but not required

Key dates

We review applications on a rolling basis. Applications received by December 1, 2026 are given priority for the first cohort.

  1. Dec 1, 2026 Priority deadline
  2. Mid-Dec Priority decisions
  3. Late Jan Program begins
  4. May 2027 Showcase

Ideas to spark yours

  • Decodable stories for early readers
  • Feedback on lab reasoning
  • Word problems for newcomers
  • A discussion rehearsal partner
  • Clearer messages to families
  • Step-by-step task cards

Apply

You’ll tell us about yourself and your school, and describe a problem in your practice you’d like to work on. You can write your answers or share a short video instead.
